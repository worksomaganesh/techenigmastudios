# Research Task — TECHENIGMASTUDIOS Episode 001

You are the lead technical research analyst for TECHENIGMASTUDIOS.

Episode: What Actually Happens When You Press Send? — Inside the Journey of a Prompt Through an LLM.

Target duration: 60 seconds.
Audience: Beginners interested in AI and technology.
Purpose: Explain the technical journey of a text prompt through a typical modern autoregressive LLM.

## Your task

Conduct evidence-backed research on these stages:

1. User input and request handling.
2. Conversation context and instruction assembly.
3. Tokenization and token IDs.
4. Token embeddings and positional information.
5. Transformer layers and self-attention.
6. Logits, probability distributions and decoding strategies.
7. Autoregressive next-token generation and final text decoding.

## Research requirements

- Prioritise original research papers, official technical documentation and authoritative engineering resources.
- Use exact source URLs. Do not invent citations.
- Cross-check important technical claims using more than one reliable source wherever possible.
- Clearly distinguish established facts from implementation-dependent behaviour.
- Explain complex concepts accurately in beginner-friendly English.
- Distinguish logits from probabilities.
- Explain that tokenisation does not necessarily split text into whole words.
- Do not imply that all LLMs or AI assistants use an identical architecture or request-processing pipeline.
- Do not assume a universal token ID, embedding dimension, number of layers or attention configuration.
- Do not write the final narration or storyboard yet.

## Required deliverables

### A. Research Brief
Explain all seven stages in their correct technical sequence.

### B. Claim–Evidence Matrix
Create a table with:
Claim ID | Technical claim | Supporting source | Exact source URL | Confidence | Caveat

Assign unique IDs such as C001, C002, C003.

### C. Visualisation Opportunities
For each stage, suggest one technically accurate visual metaphor or animation concept suitable for a premium 9:16 educational video.

### D. Accuracy and Risk Report
List:
- Common misconceptions to avoid.
- Claims that are simplified for a 60-second explanation.
- Details that vary between model providers and architectures.
- Any claims that should not appear in the episode because evidence is insufficient.

### E. Source Register
For every source, provide its title, publisher, URL, publication or update date when available, and the specific claim it supports.

Final instruction: Research first. Evidence before storytelling. Do not generate a script until the research and claim–evidence matrix are complete.
